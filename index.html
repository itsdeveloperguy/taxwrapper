HSN (Harmonised System of Nomenclature) is a standard code that classifies goods. It starts from a 6-digit international system, and India extends it to 8 digits.

It is required because the HSN decides which GST rate applies, and it must be printed on invoices and reported in GST returns. How many digits you must show depends on your turnover: generally 4 digits up to ₹5 crore and 6 digits above that (Notification 78/2020 - Central Tax).

Not sure of a code? Try our HSN Lookup.

It depends on where the goods go.

  • Within one state: you charge CGST (goes to the Centre) and SGST (goes to the state), usually half of the GST rate each. At 18% that is 9% + 9%. Union territories without a legislature charge UTGST instead of SGST.
  • Between two states: you charge only IGST, at the full rate, and it is shared between the Centre and the destination state.

The test is your location against the place of supply. For marketplace orders delivered to another state, it is IGST.

ITC lets you subtract the GST you paid on business purchases from the GST you collect on sales, so tax is paid only on the value you add.

A registered taxpayer can claim it when:

  • the goods or services are used for business,
  • you hold a valid tax invoice and have received the goods or services,
  • the supplier has filed their return and paid the tax, so the credit shows in your GSTR-2B,
  • you have filed your own return.

Credit is blocked for some items (for example personal use and certain food, vehicle and similar expenses), and composition taxpayers cannot claim it.

TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) means the person paying certain kinds of income, such as salary, rent, professional fees or commission, holds back a prescribed percentage as tax and deposits it with the government on your behalf.

It collects tax as income arises and makes it harder to avoid. The amount deducted shows in your Form 26AS and AIS, and you claim it as credit when you file your income tax return. Marketplace sellers can also see TDS and GST TCS taken from payouts. Use our TDS interest calculator to work out interest on late deduction or payment.

These rules, made under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, set duties for online marketplaces and sellers to protect buyers. In short:

  • Show seller details, the total price with a breakup, and the country of origin.
  • Give clear return, refund, exchange and warranty terms.
  • Appoint a grievance officer: acknowledge complaints within 48 hours and resolve them within one month.
  • No unfair practices such as manipulating prices, fake reviews or misleading listings.

Sellers must give accurate information to the marketplace and honour what they list. The rules have been amended and further changes have been proposed, so check the latest text.

A tax invoice is issued by a registered person who charges GST on taxable goods or services. The buyer can use it to claim ITC.

A bill of supply is issued when no GST is charged: for exempt supplies, or by a composition taxpayer. It shows no tax amount, and the buyer cannot claim ITC on it.

GSTR-1 is the statement of your sales (outward supplies), listed invoice by invoice. It is what your buyers' credit is based on.

GSTR-3B is the summary return where you report total sales, the ITC you claim, and the net tax payable. The tax is paid when you file it.

For monthly filers the usual due dates are the 11th for GSTR-1 and the 20th for GSTR-3B. Quarterly (QRMP) filers have different dates. See the Tax Calendar.

Under Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, a tax invoice carries:

  • Supplier's name, address and GSTIN.
  • A unique, consecutive invoice number and the date.
  • Buyer's name, address and GSTIN (if registered), with the state and its code.
  • Description of goods or services, HSN or SAC, quantity and unit.
  • Total value, discount, and taxable value.
  • Rate and amount of CGST, SGST or IGST (and cess if any).
  • Place of supply, and delivery address if different.
  • Whether tax is payable on reverse charge, and the supplier's signature or digital signature.
